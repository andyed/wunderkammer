## 2. The medium's history and its practitioners' vocabulary

Comparing images on a wall or panel:
- Aby Warburg, *Bilderatlas Mnemosyne* (1924–29): photographs pinned to black
  cloth panels (*Tafeln*) so images could be compared and re-arranged.
- Heinrich Wölfflin's comparative lecture with two lantern slides projected side
  by side (from the 1890s), the origin of art history's "compare and contrast".
- André Malraux, *Le Musée imaginaire* (1947), translated as "museum without
  walls": photographic reproduction lets any works be set beside each other.
- Salon and academy hanging: floor-to-ceiling hangs; a work "on the line" hangs
  at eye level, a work "skied" hangs high (Royal Academy usage). The dense
  arrangement is also called a "Petersburg hang".
- Paired works hung as "pendants"; hinged pairs as a "diptych".

Looking through many images to choose:
- Photographic contact sheets and proof sheets; the light table and the loupe;
  a photo editor's "selects".
- Magic-lantern and slide projection: diascope, episcope, epidiascope,
  stereopticon "dissolving views".

Keeping and showing a collection:
- Cabinet of curiosities (*Wunderkammer*, *Kunstkammer*); the vitrine; the
  specimen drawer and the herbarium sheet, made for side-by-side comparison of
  specimens.
- Museum roles: the docent (a guide who shows visitors a collection), the
  registrar (keeps accession records), the preparator (installs and hangs work).
- Museum "visible storage" or "open storage" galleries; the "study collection".
- Archive and library terms: stacks, finding aid, folio, plate, carrel,
  concordance, collation; Vannevar Bush, "As We May Think" (1945), the Memex.

## 3. Technical vocabulary of what the product does

- Overview, zoom, filter, details-on-demand, relate, history, extract
  (Shneiderman, "The Eyes Have It", 1996; docs/PRECEDENTS.md:12).
- Semantic zoom (Pad++, Bederson and Hollan, 1994; docs/PRECEDENTS.md:14);
  Data Mountain (Robertson et al., 1998; docs/PRECEDENTS.md:13); Perspective
  Wall (Mackinlay, Robertson and Card, 1991).
- Faceted browsing; dynamic queries (Ahlberg and Shneiderman, 1994); brushing
  and linking; small multiples (Tufte, 1983); interactive piling (Lekschas et
  al., 2021; docs/PRECEDENTS.md:30–35).
- PivotViewer and Deep Zoom (Microsoft Live Labs, 2010); docs/PEER_STUDY.md.
- Texture atlas, tile, level of detail, instancing, cohort, evidence, provenance,
  identity, scope, hand-back.
- Ekphrasis: a verbal description of a visual work (what a language model does
  when it describes the images it shows).
